# Platform safe zones (Android)

The safe zone is **a view aid in the editor only**: a switch in the editor's top bar turns it on; a small pill of three round icon buttons (no text) then appears in a strip of its own directly above the video (below the top bar; it fades in and out, and the video takes the height left, so the whole frame stays in view; the layout changes once, nothing resizes frame by frame) to pick the platform. The pill is in the theme's colours (a slightly raised surface with a thin outline, icons in the secondary text colour); the chosen icon sits on a high-contrast circle (light in the dark theme, dark in the light theme), cut out of it in the background colour. The circle slides from the old choice to the new one (a short spring of about 250 ms, following the system's animation speed, mirrored in a right-to-left layout) while the icons' colours cross-fade with it (an icon the circle passes stays visible). On a phone the strip is hidden while a panel is open in the timeline's place, and on a screen under 480 dp tall (a phone on its side); the overlay still shows there, for the platform chosen last. On a tablet with a tall video at the side, the video's width allows for the strip's height. The icons are the platforms' real logos, icon only and single-colour: TikTok's for TikTok, Instagram's for Reels and YouTube's for Shorts, taken unmodified from Simple Icons 16.38.1 (https://simpleicons.org, npm package `simple-icons`, CC0-1.0: `icons/tiktok.svg`, `icons/instagram.svg`, `icons/youtube.svg`). The logos are trademarks of their owners and are used only to label which platform's safe zone is shown. They ship as vector drawables (`res/drawable/platform_tiktok.xml`, `platform_reels.xml`, `platform_shorts.xml`: the SVG's one path on a 24x24 viewport, white, drawn tinted in the theme's colours): the owner can replace a file to change an icon without touching code (a white-on-transparent PNG of the same name in `res/drawable-nodpi` also works once the XML is removed). The switch draws a generic mock of a short-video app's own screens over the preview, inside the video frame, laid out by the platform's covered areas. It shows only when the frame being edited is vertical 9:16 (a vertical export, or a video shot at 9:16 portrait, width/height between 0.53 and 0.60; not 4:5 or a taller screen recording) and is hidden for landscape and square frames. It is never burned into the export, and it is not part of templates, template previews, batch or export placement: subtitles and the watermark are not moved by platform.

Values are fractions of a 1080x1920 frame: the part the platform's own screens cover at the top, bottom, left and right. `SafeZone.of(platform)` in `template/SafeZone.kt` holds them.

| Platform | Top | Bottom | Left | Right |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | 240 px (12.5%) | 660 px (34.4%) | 120 px (11.1%) | 180 px (16.7%, the button column) |
| Instagram Reels | 14% | 35% | 6% | 6% |
| YouTube Shorts | 288 px (15%) | 672 px (35%) | 48 px (4.4%) | 192 px (17.8%) |

TikTok and Reels are separate choices in the chooser because their zones differ at the sides (11% / 17% against 6%) and at the top.

## Sources and how sure they are

None of the three platforms publishes one official table of numbers for organic videos. The values come from their official ad templates and guides, as measured or quoted by the pages below (checked November 2026). They are a safe margin, not an exact map: the real screens change between app versions and phones.

- TikTok: TikTok for Business, "TikTok Auction In-Feed Ads" (https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/video-ads-specifications?lang=en) says the safe zone depends on the caption length and offers downloadable safe-zone templates (in-feed standard, LTR). The numbers above are measured from those templates (720x1280, scaled by 1.5) in https://www.manypixels.co/blog/social-media-design/tiktok-ad-specs . A caption of several lines covers more at the bottom (up to about 1014 px); we use the standard one.
- Instagram Reels: Meta's Reels ads guide (https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/facebook-facebook-reels) and its safe-zone guardrail: 14% top, 35% bottom, 6% at the sides, as quoted in https://billo.app/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones and https://www.hopperhq.com/blog/instagram-reel-size/ . Meta's page itself could not be fetched here (robots).
- YouTube Shorts: Google's "Universal video ad safe zones" (https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/universalsafezones-youtube.pdf), whose vertical template gives the safe rectangle; the pixel values are read from it in https://adkit.so/tools/safe-zones/youtube and https://www.tella.com/help/editing/social-safe-zones.md . See also Google Ads, "YouTube Shorts ad specs" (https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13547298).

## How the overlay works

- Overlay (`drawSafeZone` in `ui/SafeZoneMock.kt`): a generic, made-up app interface in semi-transparent white with a soft shadow, over a light shade at the top and bottom, plus a thin dashed outline around the clear middle. The overlay copies no platform's logo, name or artwork (the logos appear only on the chooser's buttons, above). Elements: a tab strip at the top ("Following | For you"; TikTok adds a search icon, Shorts search and menu), a right column of buttons placed in the right margin (TikTok: avatar with plus, heart, comment, bookmark, share, sound disc; Reels: heart, comment, paper plane, menu, sound thumbnail; Shorts: like, dislike, comment, share, menu, sound thumbnail), at the bottom the account line (Reels and Shorts with an avatar and a Follow / Subscribe pill), two caption bars, a sound line, and a thin progress bar. All positions come from the platform's margins. Drawn in the editor's canvas, over the video's window only: it takes no touches.
- `SafeZone.appliesTo(exportAspect, sourceWidth, sourceHeight)` decides whether the aid is offered.
