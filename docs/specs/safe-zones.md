# Platform safe zones (Android and web)

The safe zone is **a view aid in the editor only**: a switch in the editor's top bar turns it on; a small floating pill of three equal segments with the platforms' icons (no text) then appears over the top of the video, centred, to pick the platform. The pill takes no space of its own: the video keeps its full size whether the aid is on or off. It is dark glass (black at about 55%, a thin white outline), at most 122 dp wide and 34 dp tall, 4 dp below the video's top edge; the chosen icon sits on a light stadium, the others are light on the glass. The mock's own top elements (status line, tab strip) start below the pill, so nothing is covered awkwardly. On a phone the pill is hidden while a panel is open in the timeline's place, and on a screen under 480 dp tall (a phone on its side); the overlay itself stays. Once the switch is on the overlay stays visible while editing, with or without the pill, until it is turned off; the switch and the platform are saved per project (the app: `safeZone` in project.json; the web: `safe` in the project's saved state) and come back when the project is opened again. The platform alone is also remembered for new projects. On switching, the overlay glides from the old platform's margins to the new one's (260 ms, easing out). The icons are the platforms' real logos, icon only and single-colour, taken unmodified from Simple Icons 16.38.1 (https://simpleicons.org, CC0-1.0); they are trademarks of their owners and are used only to label which platform's safe zone is shown. They are unchanged from the earlier chooser. The overlay itself draws no platform's logo, name or artwork. It shows only when the frame being edited is vertical 9:16 (a vertical export, or a video shot at 9:16 portrait, width/height between 0.53 and 0.60; not 4:5 or a taller screen recording) and is hidden for landscape and square frames. It is never burned into the export, and it is not part of templates, template previews, batch or export placement: subtitles and the watermark are not moved by platform. The web's export worker does not import the mock (`web/tests/parity2.cjs`).

Values are fractions of a 1080x1920 frame: the part the platform's own screens cover at the top, bottom, left and right. `SafeZone.of(platform)` in `template/SafeZone.kt` holds them.

| Platform | Top | Bottom | Left | Right |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | 240 px (12.5%) | 660 px (34.4%) | 120 px (11.1%) | 300 px (27.8%: the 120 px margin plus the 180 px button column) |
| Instagram Reels | 14% | 35% | 6% | 6% |
| YouTube Shorts | 288 px (15%) | 672 px (35%) | 48 px (4.4%) | 192 px (17.8%) |

TikTok and Reels are separate choices in the chooser because their zones differ at the sides (11% / 28% against 6%) and at the top.

## Sources and how sure they are

None of the three platforms publishes one official table of numbers for organic videos. The values come from their official ad templates and guides, as measured or quoted by the pages below (re-checked 2026-11-27). They are a safe margin, not an exact map: the real screens change between app versions and phones.

- TikTok: TikTok for Business, "TikTok Auction In-Feed Ads" (https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/video-ads-specifications?lang=en) says the safe zone depends on the caption length and offers downloadable safe-zone templates (in-feed standard, LTR). The numbers above are measured from those templates (720x1280, scaled by 1.5) in https://www.manypixels.co/blog/social-media-design/tiktok-ad-specs . Re-checked: the standard template keeps 120 px clear at each side, and below y=840 the right side also holds the 180 px button column (so 300 px there; the earlier 180 px forgot the margin). A caption of several lines (the anchor templates) covers more at the bottom (up to about 1014 px); we use the standard one.
- Instagram Reels: Meta's Reels ads guide (https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/facebook-facebook-reels) and its safe-zone guardrail: 14% top, 35% bottom, 6% at the sides, as quoted in https://billo.app/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones and https://www.hopperhq.com/blog/instagram-reel-size/ . Meta's page itself could not be fetched here (robots). Its action rail (lower right) is wider than 6%: Meta asks for 40% clear at the bottom there; the mock draws the rail inside the margin and the numbers stay at 6%.
- YouTube Shorts: Google's "Universal video ad safe zones" (https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/universalsafezones-youtube.pdf; the PDF itself could not be fetched here), whose vertical template gives the safe rectangle; the pixel values are read from it in https://adkit.so/tools/safe-zones/youtube and https://www.tella.com/help/editing/social-safe-zones.md . See also Google Ads, "YouTube Shorts ad specs" (https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13547298).

## How the overlay works

- Overlay (`drawSafeZone` in `ui/SafeZoneMock.kt`, and `lib/safezonemock.ts` on the web; both take a `reserved` height at the top for the pill): a generic, made-up app interface in semi-transparent white with a soft shadow, over a light shade at the top and bottom, plus a thin dashed outline around the clear middle. The overlay copies no platform's logo, name or artwork (the logos appear only on the chooser's buttons, above). Elements: a tab strip at the top ("Following | For you"; TikTok adds a search icon, Shorts search and menu), a right column of buttons placed in the right margin (TikTok: avatar with plus, heart, comment, bookmark, share, sound disc; Reels: heart, comment, paper plane, menu, sound thumbnail; Shorts: like, dislike, comment, share, menu, sound thumbnail), at the bottom the account line (Reels and Shorts with an avatar and a Follow / Subscribe pill), two caption bars, a sound line, and a thin progress bar. All positions come from the platform's margins. Drawn in the editor's canvas, over the video's window only: it takes no touches.
- `SafeZone.appliesTo(exportAspect, sourceWidth, sourceHeight)` decides whether the aid is offered.
